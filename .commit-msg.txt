Draw the contract the way Word does, not as plain text

The preview showed the contract's text with all its formatting thrown
away. Which meant the article numbers were missing entirely: "제1조" is
not text in the document, it's generated from numbering.xml, so 274
numbered paragraphs came out unnumbered. The centred cover, bold defined
terms, indentation showing which sub-clause belongs to which clause, and
all 14 tables were gone too.

So it now reads the formatting out of the .docx instead of only the text:

- lib/xml.ts - a small XML reader. Hand-written rather than a dependency
  because this project's whole subject is confidential company
  documents, and every package added is one more thing that gets to see
  them. OOXML is machine-generated valid XML; elements, attributes and
  text is a small enough job to own.
- lib/docx-layout.ts - paragraphs, runs, list numbering resolved the way
  Word resolves it, tables, page setup. Scoped to what this contract
  actually contains, surveyed first with scripts/survey-formatting.mts
  and survey-numbering.mts, so nothing is approximated on spec alone.
- contract-view.tsx - draws it on an A4 sheet at the document's own
  margins (70.9pt, read from its sectPr), in 맑은 고딕, with the slot
  highlighting and Ctrl+F-style jumping kept.

What it deliberately does not do is paginate. Word decides where pages
end by measuring text; this is one continuous sheet with the document's
three explicit page breaks marked. The .docx download remains the
document of record.

Two details worth keeping in mind:

- The list marker uses min-width, not width. "제1조" is wider than its
  20pt hanging indent, and a fixed width let the clause text run back
  over the top of it. Word moves the text to the next tab stop.
- @page margin is now 0, because the sheet already carries the
  document's margins and the printer was adding its own on top. The
  screens built around <main> get theirs from a print rule instead.

Checked with scripts/check-layout.mts (85/85 slots reachable, all 35
article numbers in order, no text lost, 14 tables) and
scripts/render-contract.mts, which renders the component to a standalone
HTML file - the only way to confirm it draws without a browser to look
at.
